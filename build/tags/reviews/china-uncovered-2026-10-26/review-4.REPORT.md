# China uncovered-table review 4

Reviewed all **95 complete tables**. Every key is accounted for: **78** have existing-tag fits, **5** are deferred, and **24** support one or more candidate proposals. Candidate evidence overlaps existing fits where a proposal is only a useful refinement.

No assignments or vocabulary were changed.

## Strongest additions

- **anthropometry** — four tables of cephalic indices or bodily-trait classification. The term should index the historical source without endorsing its racial typology.
- **legal-profession** — three tables of certified lawyers or advocate licences.
- **public-holidays-and-commemorations** — two recurring lists of Republic commemorative observances. Clarifying the current `calendars-and-time` definition is a reasonable alternative to another tag.

## Optional or conditional additions

- **route-distances** — eleven route-distance matrices, including mixed sea/overland routes. Most maritime examples already fit `shipping`, which explicitly includes sea routes.
- **militias-and-volunteer-forces** — three recurring Shanghai Volunteer Corps tables. `army` already covers land forces generally, so this is optional precision.
- **riots-and-civil-unrest** — one casualty table under the recorded running section “The Korean Riots.” Confirm that causal link before assigning; do not infer military casualties from the book's invasion chapter.

## Existing vocabulary was often interpreted too narrowly

- `silver` explicitly includes bullion and silver trade; combine it with `imports`/`exports`.
- `foreign-exchange` includes fixed currency conversion, not just market quotations.
- `public-debt` includes government borrowing liabilities and treasury debt, not only bonds.
- `banking` explicitly includes bank accounts; `companies` explicitly includes a single company's balance sheets and profits.
- `taxation` explicitly includes assessment; `imperial-household` explicitly includes succession.
- `river-conservancy` includes conservancy boards, and `vocational-education` includes professional schools.
- `industrial-output` and `mineral-production` are available when an exact industry or mineral term is absent.
- Multiple detailed tags can represent a genuinely mixed table; it does not require one invented term that encompasses every column.

The Kwangchouwan directory and Mongol-group inventory also need their existing `text-tables` flags reviewed because the body includes substantive personnel/facility or administrative-unit counts. These are advisory notes, not changes.

## Deferred

- `china-1926-7:p0774_1`: The excerpt gives walnut quantities traded from provinces but does not establish whether the relevant trade boundary is domestic or international. Horticulture is a possible commodity-subject reuse if its definition is clarified to cover orchard commodity trade; adding a walnut ID would not resolve the trade-direction uncertainty.
- `china-1929-30:p0348_1`: One textual table describes rival governmental/military groups and territorial control. This could justify a political-fragmentation or political-and-military-factions term if similar material recurs elsewhere, but army, parties and formal administrative-divisions should not be forced onto the whole table. Defer a new narrow ID pending cross-batch evidence.
- `china-1929-30:p0971_1`: This is the geographical allocation of a 50-farm survey sample, without substantive farm size, tenure, output or household measures. A methodological agricultural-surveys term would describe it, but a one-off sample-distribution table is weak evidence for expanding the subject vocabulary.
- `china-1931-2:p0234_1`: The columns lack both readable year headings and direction/total labels. Existing imports/exports would be speculative; no new vocabulary term can recover missing source context.
- `china-1950:p2-0066_1`: Iron production is not identified as ore versus smelted iron in the complete frozen table. Do not add an ambiguous iron catch-all merely to fill the slot; clarify the source measure before selecting iron-ore or iron-and-steel.
