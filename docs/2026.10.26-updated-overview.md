## What we have been doing

- **The site.** `stat-tables-site` (GitHub Pages, deployed from `main`) is a static site of statistical tables,
  directories and chronologies transcribed from yearbooks on Japan, Korea, Taiwan, Manchuria, China and the
  South Seas Mandate. `build/build_site.py` builds it from each book's `<Book>/_work/` JSON. There are about 140
  BOOKS entries and about 45,000 tables in `data/`. This session runs the repo and pushes finished books to `main`
  itself (user, 2026-10-06).
- **Books finished since late September.** Japan Year Book 1935. 朝鮮年鑑 1926 and 1940. 朝鮮事情 1935, 1940 and
  1942–44. The Manchuria progress reports and the Manchukuo Directory 1938-9. All the English Korea annual
  reports from 1911 to 1939. The China Year Books and Handbooks. The Japan Year Books 1906–1943, the
  Japan-Manchoukuo Year Books 1934–1940 and the Manchuria/Manchoukuo Year Books. 統監府統計年報 1908,
  朝鮮總督府統計年報 1939, 臺灣統計書 1925/1933/1938/1942, 大日本帝國統計年鑑 Nos. 54, 57 and 59 (Hitotsubashi
  images, credited) and 南洋廳統計年鑑 Nos. 2 and 7.
- **Site features added from 10-12 to 10-24.**
  - A printed contents (`toc.json`) for every book, with scan links and "Include table links [T]" (on by default).
  - Doubtful-cell (`[?]`) statistics on the book cards.
  - Cross-edition links (statistical yearbooks).
  - Topic pages (population, education, religion, crime) and a Table / Chart / Both view for every table
    (`charts.js`, our own code).
  - Totals on the home page, and a collapsible table list.
  - Pagefind search that works from a subfolder.
- **Two production methods.**
  1. *Agent missions* (Claude Code sub-agents). Used for Japanese and dense books and for the Japan-Manchoukuo
     Year Books. The steps are contact-sheet survey → missions of 3–6 pages → `tables/`, `directory/` and
     `chronology/` JSON → FIXES → normalise → toc → translations → publish.
  2. *The `llm` CLI pipeline* (`_pipeline/`). Used for the long English yearbooks. A Sonnet survey works from
     contact sheets (it also finds rotated pages and spreads), then Haiku extracts each page through the `llm`
     CLI with Claude models (`run.py` runs it in parallel), then `cyb_assemble.py` assembles the results.
     **CLAUDE.md does not describe this method yet.**

## How the approach changed (most recent decisions win)

- **Models.** Opus did all the reading in September; it clearly beat Sonnet on dense kanji tables. From
  10-07/08 the user wants **Sonnet** for missions, surveys and checks. From 10-13 **Haiku** handles easy English
  missions with at most one dense page (99.4 % agreement in the trial). **Opus** is kept for sideways pages,
  spreads, 3+ dense pages and dense Japanese 目次. The "Opus for reading" line in CLAUDE.md is out of date.
- **Checking was cut back for cost.**
  - The 10 % random check pass was dropped.
  - Blind re-reading now covers only cells that were changed after a total check.
  - Opus spot-checks gave 99.7–100 % agreement on the NDL statistical yearbooks. After that the user said "no
    more opus tests". For 統計年報 1939 the user said: "No elaborate check rounds after you finish".
- **Contents come first** (user, 10-14). A dedicated pass to locate headings across JMYB 1939 used up a week of
  quota. Now the contents are transcribed before the survey. Survey agents and mission reports record the
  headings they see, titles are fuzzy-matched locally, and only the leftovers go to one small agent. An entry
  that cannot be placed links to its chapter.
- **Chronologies** are transcribed in every book except the Japan Year Books. The llm pipeline had been skipping
  them by mistake.
- **Transcription rules** have not changed. No OCR. `[?]` means unreadable and `""` means empty in the original.
  A printed total settles only a 3-vs-8 digit or a 一/二/三 grouping. Never correct the source. New books get
  `blank_marking: true`.
- **Charts** draw printed figures only. Gaps are never filled, labels keep their printed order, printed totals
  are marked, and mismatches show ≠. The user sees these tables as historical constructions that students
  should be able to question.
- **Housekeeping rules.**
  - Run heavy jobs with `nice -n 15` and cores − 3 threads, one at a time.
  - Copy downloaded scans to Oma (`/Volumes/Oma/Book Scans/...`) before deleting them.
  - After a book is published, delete its work images and its sub-agent transcripts.
  - Rebuild the search index only after a series is finished.
  - When a mission finishes, reply only "N done, M left".

## Recent problems and lessons

- **Quota.** A whole-book locate pass and repeated Opus checks cost more than they found. Prefer passes that
  reuse earlier output and are limited to the pages that need them.
- **Coarse scans.** 朝鮮年鑑 1941 was abandoned after 12 of 50 missions because about 68 % of cells came out
  blank at 5068 px per spread. Low-resolution NDL scans give a few % `[?]` (Taiwan 1942: about 2,460 of 109k).
  Check a pilot's blank rate before committing to a book.
- **Sonnet and contents pages.** Sonnet guesses smudged page numbers and calls its own misreads "misprints".
  Send its "uncertain" or "out of sequence" items back for a tight-crop re-read.
- **Disk.** The disk filled on 10-09. Sub-agent transcripts reached 49 GB by 10-21 (about 100 MB per mission,
  because every crop is embedded). Today there are 77 GB free.
- **Git.**
  - `push.default=upstream` once pushed a branch straight to `main`. Always branch with `--no-track` and push
    with an explicit refspec.
  - The git directory of this worktree is on **/Volumes/Oma**, so git fails if Oma is not mounted.
  - Revert other books' incidental rebuilt files before committing.
- **Shell.** `cp` and `mv` are aliased to interactive mode. A background `mv` hung on an overwrite prompt
  today. Use `\cp -f` and `\mv -f`.
- **Crop cleanup globs.** Today `glob('crops/M_p1-021*')` also matched another mission's folder
  (`M_p1-0215`). Delete only exact, known file names.
- **The `llm` pipeline path.** `_pipeline/pipeline.sh` and `myb_pipeline.sh` call `$H/../sheets.py`, but
  `sheets.py` is now in `_pipeline/` itself. Fix the path before running them again.
