# Finder pages: the Population page (added 2026-11-07, version 1.118)

An overview of the feature: what it is for, how a visitor uses it, how it is built, and how it is kept up to date.
The working rules are in `CLAUDE.md` ("Finder pages", workflow step 17); this file explains them.

## Purpose

A visitor who wants one figure — the population of Keijō in 1930, of Taiwan in 1905, of Japanese residents in
Kwantung — should not have to know which of 177 books and 61,000 tables holds it. A **finder page** gathers one
tightly defined kind of figure from every table on the site, place by place and year by year, and keeps every claim:
where several tables print the same figure it is shown once with a link to each; where they disagree, the figures
stand side by side. Population is the first; others will follow the same pattern.

## What the visitor sees

- **Where:** `figures/population.html`, listed on the hub `figures/index.html`. Linked from the Topics index (button
  beside "Tables with Map"), from a banner on the population topic page, the regional population pages and the
  population tags (totals, households, nationality and ethnicity, overseas communities, age and sex, household
  registration, census methods, migration). By the author's decision it is **not** linked from the home page.
- **Finding a place:** a search box (English, CJK, or any name as printed: Keijō, 京城, Kwangtung, Tokyo-fu,
  Saipan), "Try" links, and region tiles (Japanese Empire, Japan proper, Korea, Taiwan, Karafuto, Kwantung, South
  Seas, Manchuria, China, other countries) to drill down through provinces, prefectures, cities, districts and wards.
- **A place:** breadcrumb; chips for the count (population, male, female, households) and the group (everyone,
  Japanese, Korean, Chinese, foreigners, islanders …); a dot chart by year (dots are never joined — gaps stay gaps;
  hollow dots mark figures whose table gives no date; shaded years have differing figures); then one row per year.
  Each figure shows its date and basis where the tables give them ("31 Dec · registered", "1 Oct · census") and a
  link ("pill") to every table that prints it, by book and page; "details" gives each table's title, the date as
  stated, the basis, notes and the place name as printed. Differing figures show the gap in %. A figure printed
  rounded that agrees with another to the precision printed is shown as "≈ same, rounded", not as a conflict.
  Below: the places within it, with their latest figure.
- **Into the tables:** each pill opens the book page at the table with the row and the cell marked
  (`book/<slug>/#<table id>~<part>.<row>.<col>`).
- **From elsewhere:** table pages whose figures are on the page have a "Population across all books →" button;
  Search shows a card for a matching place ("Population: Keijō 京城 — 192 figures, 1906–1939"); the Japanese Empire
  map's place card can link to the place (written, not yet released — see below); About and `llms.txt` describe it.

At launch: 123,816 figures from 1,537 tables, about 8,000 places.

## How it is built

**Nothing is re-read from the scans.** Every figure is a cell of a transcribed table, copied as transcribed.

1. **Candidates.** `make_previews.py` picks the tables through the controlled tags (`config.json`: population totals,
   households, nationality and ethnicity, overseas communities, household registration, census methods; plus title
   and column words in population, geography and government tables) — 2,853 tables.
2. **Specs.** Sonnet agents read compact previews of each table (`TASK.md`) and wrote a short spec: which rows and
   columns hold counts, for which place (as printed), date, group and basis, multiplier for figures in thousands, or
   `skip` with a reason (1,272 skipped: births, rates, occupations …). Six agents did the first pass.
3. **Extraction.** `extract.py` follows each spec and takes the cells: figures with commas, points used as
   thousands separators and kanji numerals are read; blanks, `[?]`, dashes and ditto marks give nothing. Dates come
   from the spec or from year labels (Western years, 明治/大正/昭和/民國 eras, ditto rows); a table with no date gets
   its edition year, marked "date not stated".
4. **Places.** Printed names are matched to a gazetteer frozen from the Japanese Empire map
   (`places/places.json`: prefectures, Korean provinces, Taiwanese 州/廳 and 郡, Manchukuo and Chinese provinces,
   South Seas groups and islands, cities, countries), within the region the table is about (a Korean town printed
   "Koshū" is not Hangzhou), at the same level (a county is not the town of the same name; a ward is not its city).
   Hand matches and new places are in `places/aliases.json`. Names still unmatched stay as printed, under their region.
5. **Checks.** `check.py` lists figures far from their neighbours (under ¼ or over 4× the median nearby); an agent
   reviewed the worst and corrected 29 specs (`spec_fixes.json`: a percentage column read as people, families read
   as people, "other foreigners" read as all foreigners …). A misprint in a book stays as printed.
6. **Page.** `build_population.py` writes `data/population/index.json` (places, sources) and one file per region,
   which the page loads as needed, and `table_links.json` for the table-page buttons. `build/finders/build_all.py`
   runs every finder and the hub at the end of `build_site.py`.

## Keeping it up to date

- **Every build** re-extracts from the current data, so corrections to a table reach the page automatically.
- **Each spec carries a fingerprint** of its table; a table that has been re-transcribed or rebuilt is left out
  until it gets a new spec, so a spec never reads the wrong cells.
- **Finder batch, every 5–10 new books or monthly** (not per book): build; `python3 build/finders/status.py
  --batches` lists tables with no spec, changed tables, new unmatched place names and new suspicious figures, and
  writes batch files; one Sonnet agent per finder writes the specs, one handles the new place names
  (`places/ALIAS_TASK.md`), one the new suspicious figures (`OUTLIER_TASK.md`); build again; look at a few new
  places; `status.py --baseline`; commit, bump the version, push. A new table's button on its book page appears one
  build after its spec.

## Known limits

- Matching and dating were done by machine; about 350 tables still have flagged figures, most of them genuine
  (fast-growing groups, changed city boundaries), some spec mistakes nobody has reviewed.
- Many small towns (Taiwanese 街庄, Korean 郡, Chinese counties) stay as printed; a few bare names are ambiguous
  (東區, Koshu, "India").
- Japanese cities are listed under Japan proper, not their prefectures: the gazetteer has no prefecture membership.
- A few tables were dated by agents from a matching total in another table (`korea-1944` p0017–18,
  `japan-tokei-1935` p0043/p0048); these dates should be treated as approximate.
