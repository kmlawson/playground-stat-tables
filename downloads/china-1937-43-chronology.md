## The Kuomintang — Chronology of the Kuomintang

**1892** Dr. Sun formed the first Hsin Chung Hui in Macao. — p. 43 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1893** Dr. Sun was kidnapped and held in the Manchu Legation in London. — p. 43 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1894** Hsin Chung Hui was formed in Honolulu. — p. 43 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1895** Hsin Chung Hui was formed in Hongkong and the United States. In September Dr. Sun engendered the first revolutionary uprising in Canton. It failed and Dr. Sun began his life of exile, first going to Japan and later to America. — p. 43 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1898** Dr. Sun returned to Japan. He sent agents to contact secret societies along the Yangtze and in the southern provinces of Kwangtung, Kwangsi, and Fukien. — p. 43 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1900** Dr. Sun organized two revolts in Kwangtung, one in Waichow and the other in Canton. — p. 43 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1902** Revolutionary revolt in Canton. — p. 43 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1904** Revolutionary revolt at Changsha. — p. 43 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1905** That spring Dr. Sun went to Europe, meeting Chinese revolutionaries in Brussels, Paris, and Berlin. In the summer he was in Japan lecturing on the Three People's Principles and the Quintuple-Power Constitution. On August 20, under Dr. Sun's leadership, the Tung Men Hui was formed in Tokyo, absorbing the Hsin Chung Hui and numerous secret societies, including the San Ho Hui (Three Harmony Society) and the Ko Lao Hui (Society of Brothers and Elders), two groups having large followings in the lower social strata in China. — p. 43 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1905-1911** No less than 13 abortive revolts organized by the Tung Men Hui were staged in various parts of China. On March 12, occurred the most daring revolt in Canton. On October 10, the Wuhan Revolution took place. — p. 43 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1912** On January 1, Dr. Sun assumed the post of provisional president of the Republic of China in Nanking. — p. 43 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1912** Sung Chiao-jen, Dr. Sun's revolutionary associate, formed the Kuomintang by enlarging the scope of the Tung Men Hui to include members of four other parties. Many Tung Men Hui members who were opposed to the merger, seceded and formed a Tung Men Hui club in Shanghai to retain the revolutionary character of the organization. — p. 43 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1913** On March 20, Sung Chiao-jen was assassinated by Yuan Shih-kai's agents in Shanghai. The Second Revolution, led by Tung Men Hui revolutionaries against Yuan Shih-kai, took place and failed. — p. 43 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1914** On March 6, Dr. Sun re-organized the Kuo Min Tang into the Chung Hua Ke Min Tang in Tokyo with himself as Tsungli, or president of the Party. — p. 43 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1915-1916** When Yuan Shih-kai made himself emperor, Dr. Sun organized widespread revolts against him. — p. 43 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1917** Dr. Sun became generalissimo of a military government in Canton in opposition to the northern government. — p. 43 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1919** In October, Dr. Sun changed the name of the Party from Chung Hua Ke Min Tang into Chung KuoKuomintang. — p. 43 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1922** Chen Chiung-ming revolted in Canton. — p. 43 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1924** The Chung Kuo Kuomintang was re-organized. Chinese communists were allowed to join the Kuomintang. The same year the First National Congress of the Kuomintang was held in Canton. — p. 43 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1925** On March 12, Dr. Sun died in Peking where he had gone in October the previous year to recuperate his health and to bring about unification of the country. The same year the National Government was established in Canton. — p. 43-44 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1926** On July 9, Chiang Kai-shek was appointed commander-in-chief of the Revolutionary Forces, and launched the Northern Expedition. The Second Party National Congress was held. — p. 44 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1927** In April, Nanking was made the national capital of China. A party purge was enforced. — p. 44 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1928** The National Government was removed to Nanking. — p. 44 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1929** The Third Party National Congress was held in Nanking. The period of military operations ended and the period of political tutelage began. — p. 44 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1931** A People's Convention was held in Nanking. It adopted the Provisional Constitution. (The Japanese attack on Mukden took place on September 18.) The Fourth Party National Congress was held the same year. — p. 44 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1935** The Fifth Party National Congress was held. — p. 44 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1936** The draft Permanent Constitution was promulgated. — p. 44 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1937** The Sino-Japanese war began. — p. 44 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))

**1938** In April, an extraordinary Party National Congress was convened in Hankow. Generalissimo Chiang Kai-shek was elected Tsungtsai, or director-general, of the party. The Program of Armed Resistance and National Reconstruction was adopted. The San Min Chu I Youth Corps was organized. The People's Political Council was formed. — p. 44 ([scan part 1, leaf 34](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%201%20of%205/page/n34/mode/1up))


## Chapter XXIII: Chronology of Major Events — Chronology of Major Events

It was explained, however, that China was not formally invoking the Nine-Power Treaty. — p. 849 ([scan part 5, leaf 46](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%205%20of%205/page/n46/mode/1up))

General Sung ordered his 29th Army to resist the Japanese. — p. 849 ([scan part 5, leaf 46](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%205%20of%205/page/n46/mode/1up))

**1937, July 29** Fighting broke out in Tientsin before dawn. Nankai University was destroyed. At Nanyuan, south of Peiping, General Chao Teng-yu, commander of the 132nd Division, and General Tung Ling-ke, deputy commander of the 29th Army, were killed in action. — p. 849 ([scan part 5, leaf 46](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%205%20of%205/page/n46/mode/1up))

Governors and leaders from Shansi, Shantung, Kwangtung, Hunan, Szechwan, Yunnan and other places also gathered in or were on way to Nanking to confer with the central authorities. — p. 849 ([scan part 5, leaf 46](https://archive.org/details/china-handbook-1937-1943/China%20Handbook%201937-1943%20Part%205%20of%205/page/n46/mode/1up))
