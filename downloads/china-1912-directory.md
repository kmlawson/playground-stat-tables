## Mines and Mining: Mines and Companies

### Foreign Concessions (Coal)

**1. The Chinese Engineering and Mining Company, Limited** (British).—This company owns the Kaiping Collieries at Kaiping and Tongshan. in Chihli province, on the Imperial Railways of North China, 80 miles from Tientsin. The mines were opened in 1878 and transferred to the present company in 1900. Average daily capacity:—Tongshan Mine, 2700 tons; Linsi, 2700 tons; North-west shaft, Tongshan, 600 tons; total, 6000 tons. The Company's annual returns are also given as follows:— Head Office, Tientsin.—Agent and General Manager, Major W. S. Nathan, R.E.; Assistant-Agent, P. S. Thornton; Chief Accountant, F. A. Hanisch. Branches—Chinwangtao, Shanghai, Hongkong, London. Agencies.—Chefoo, Tsingtao, Weihaiwei, Newchwang, Mukden, Hankow. — p. 51 ([scan leaf 90](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n90/mode/1up))

**2. Pekin Syndicate, Limited** —Owning coal mines at Chinghuachen, Honan province. Opened in 1905; output began in 1908. Daily capacity, 2000 tons. Railway line from Chinghuachen to Sinsiang on the Peking-Hankow Railway. Head Office, 110 Cannon Street, London. Head Office in China, Tientsin; Agent-General, J. R. Brazier; Commercial Export Manager, H. P. King; Engineer-in-Chief, at Jameisen, Honan, J. P. Kenrick. By an agreement, signed in Peking, January 21, 1908, the Syndicate renounced its concessions in Shansi to the Bureau of Trade of Shansi for a monetary payment of Huapao Tls. 2,750,000. The third and final instalment of this amount was paid in two sums on May 9, 1910, and April 21, 1911, and was equivalent to £116,373 14s. 7d. — p. 51 ([scan leaf 90](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n90/mode/1up))

**3. Schantung Bergbau Gesellschaft** (Shangtung Mining Company). German. Collieries at Fangtze, Weihsien district (102 miles from Tsingtao), and at Hungshan, Poshan district (168 miles from Tsingtao). Opened in 1902. Employees in 1910 numbered 77 Europeans, 5800 Chinese. Revenue, after deducting expenses for management and output, is given as — p. 52 ([scan leaf 91](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n91/mode/1up))

**4. South Manchuria Railway Company** (Japanese), owning the Fushun collieries at Fushun, near Mukden, connected by railway with Suchiatun on the South Manchuria Railway. Operated from April 1, 1907. Estimated daily capacity, 3000 tons. Head Office—South Manchuria Railway Company (Mining Department), Tairen. — p. 52 ([scan leaf 91](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n91/mode/1up))

**Syndicat du Yunnan, Limited** An Anglo-French company, known as the Syndicat du Yunnan, Limited, holds a concession from the Chinese Government, dated June 21, 1902, to exploit the mineral deposits in the prefectures of Yunnanfu, Chengchiang, Liuan, Kaihua, Chouhsiang, and the departments of Yuanchiangchow, and Yungpeiting. These deposits are stated in the concession agreement to consist of copper, gold, silver, coal, iron, platinum, nickle, tin, petroleum, cinnabar, and precious stones. Owing to local opposition the exploitation of this concession has been delayed. According to the recent report of a French engineer, quoted in the press, the mines of copper and gold in Yunnan are excellent, but coal is not abundant and not of good quality. The tin mines are abundant at Liuan and Shaotung, whilst at Tali and Yungchang there are many copper mines; at Kaihua, Yungpeiting and Tunchuan there are also excellent gold and silver deposits. — p. 52 ([scan leaf 91](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n91/mode/1up))

**The Eastern Pioneer Company** The Eastern Pioneer Company (British) was formed in 1899 with a capital of £300,000 to exploit a mining concession in Szechuan granted by the Chinese Government. The concession has not been worked, and it is understood that a claim has been lodged against the Chinese authorities. — p. 52 ([scan leaf 91](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n91/mode/1up))

### Chinese Coal Mines

**1. Lanchow Collieries** Chihli province, on the Imperial Railways of North China, 83 miles from Tientsin. Modern machinery: German engineers. Average daily capacity, 4000 to 5000 tons. Output over 1000 tons daily. Head Office—Tientsin. — p. 53 ([scan leaf 92](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n92/mode/1up))

**2. Pao Chin Collieries** at Pingtingchou, Eastern Shansi, on the Chengting-Taiyuanfu Railway. Owners, the Pao Chin Mining Company of Shansi. — p. 53 ([scan leaf 92](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n92/mode/1up))

**3. To Li Mines** west of Changhsien, on Peking-Hankow Railway, 15 miles south-west of Peking. Owned by a Chinese syndicate, which is constructing an overhead railway from the mines to station at Changsintien. This line is at present transporting 200 tons per diem and, when completed, will be able to carry 1000 tons per diem. — p. 53 ([scan leaf 92](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n92/mode/1up))

**4. Lin Sheng Mines** 11 miles by branch line from Yakouying, on Peking-Hankow Railway, near Peking. Chinese company; French manager. Modern mining machinery. Two shafts, 4·80 metres in diameter and 190 metres deep, were being sunk in 1911, each with an estimated capacity for 2000 tons of coal in ten hours. Output, 500 to 800 tons per day. — p. 53 ([scan leaf 92](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n92/mode/1up))

**5. Liu Ho Kou Mine** on light railway, 15 miles west of Fenglochen, on the Peking-Hankow Railway, Chihli province. Output, 200 to 250 tons per day. — p. 53 ([scan leaf 92](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n92/mode/1up))

**6. Pinghsiang Mines** at Pinghsiang, Kiangsi province, connected by rail (65 miles) with Chuchou (on the future Canton-Hankow Railway) on the Hsiang River, 294 miles by water from Hankow. Owned by the Han Yeh Ping Iron and Coal Company (Chinese). Mines opened in 1898; under German supervision. Average daily output to July 1909, 1500 tons; since July 1909, 2000 tons. — p. 53 ([scan leaf 92](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n92/mode/1up))

**7. Poshan Mines** The Chinese owned mines in the Poshan Valley, 70 miles east of Tsinanfu, capital of Shantung, are said to have produced 250,000 tons of coal in 1910. The valley, which is about 20 miles long, and from a few hundred yards wide at one end to six miles in width in the north-west, is described as one vast coal-bed, which radiates out in different directions where the coal-bed, after being interrupted, is continued. — p. 54 ([scan leaf 93](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n93/mode/1up))

**8. Kueichou Mines** at Siangki, Hupeh province, 30 miles from the Yangtze and 80 miles above Ichang. Worked by native methods. Connected with the Yangtze by small navigable streams. Estimated monthly output, 400 tons. — p. 54 ([scan leaf 93](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n93/mode/1up))

**9. Lung Wang Tung Mines** near Chungking, Szechuan. Formerly owned by the Kiang Pei Ting Mining Company (British), now in the hands of the Chiang Ho Mining Company (Chinese), and worked by native methods. — p. 54 ([scan leaf 93](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n93/mode/1up))

### Iron

**1. Tayeh Iron Mines** at Tiehshanpu, 17 miles by light railway from Huangshihkang on the Yangtze, 70 miles below Hankow, in Hupeh province. Owned and managed by the Han Yeh Ping Iron and Coal Company, Limited (Chinese). Described as "one of the richest iron mines in the world." "Ore exposed on surface . . . estimated at 500,000,000 tons." Under Japanese supervision; men employed, 3000. — p. 54 ([scan leaf 93](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n93/mode/1up))

**2. Tungkuan Shan Mines** Tungling district on the Yangtze, 55 miles above Wuhu, Anhui province. A concession to work these mines, granted to the London and China Syndicate (British) in 1904, was surrendered in 1910 for the sum of £52,000, and the mines were transferred to a Chinese company to be formed for their exploitation. — p. 54 ([scan leaf 93](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n93/mode/1up))

### Copper

**1. Government smelting works at Yaokai, Kansu** 70 miles west of Lanchow, have been established to deal with copper ore mined in the Tsinyuan district. Machinery of American and British make. Works in charge of British engineer, who has four Belgian and Spanish assistants. Plant said to be capable of turning out 4 tons of copper in 24 hours from 7½ per cent of ores. A modern gold milling plant (British) has been erected at the same place. A tubular boiler and pump were ordered in Germany. — p. 54 ([scan leaf 93](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n93/mode/1up))

**2. Government mines at Ch'anq Pailing** Kanchow prefecture of Kiangsi province. Have been worked for many years by native methods. Inspected by German engineer in 1908, who reported very favourably both as to quantity and quality of ore. Samples were obtained containing as much as 40 per cent of copper. — p. 55 ([scan leaf 94](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n94/mode/1up))

**3. Government mines, at Pai Shui Ho** Penghsien district, Szechuan province. Three Japanese assayists engaged and a smelting furnace purchased, September, 1910. — p. 55 ([scan leaf 94](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n94/mode/1up))

**4. Government mines, in Huilichow** Ningyuan prefecture, Szechuan province. Red and white copper, worked by native methods. These mines are the chief source of supply for the province of Szechuan. Price of red copper slabs at Chengtu, 0·38 taels (11d.) per catty (1⅓ lbs.); white copper, 0·70 taels (1s. 9d.) per catty. — p. 55 ([scan leaf 94](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n94/mode/1up))

### Quicksilver

**The cinnabar mines** The cinnabar mines, at Yuanshanchiang, 15 miles from Lungchikow, in Kueichow province. A concession to work these mines was acquired by a syndicate called the Anglo-French Quicksilver and Mining Concession (Kueichow province) of China, Limited, in 1898, but owing to difficulties with the Chinese authorities little progress has been made with the development of the properties, which include iron smelting works at Tsingki, Eastern Kueichow, and various mining rights in the same province. Samples of the Yuanshanchiang ore assayed by an expert give a maximum of 4·4 per cent mercury and a minimum of 1·7 per cent; average of assays 2·78 per cent mercury. Average estimated daily output of mines 150 tons (of 2204 lbs.). London office of company—54 New Broad Street, E.C. — p. 55 ([scan leaf 94](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n94/mode/1up))

### Tin

**1. The Kochiu mines** 30 miles from Mengtze, Yunnan province. Worked by native methods; 30,000 men employed; 4216 tons, valued at £512,968, exported in 1909. A blast furnace for these mines, purchased in Germany, arrived at Haiphong in January, 1911. NOTE.—The above mines were included in a concession granted to the Yunnan Syndicate, Limited. The syndicate, in 1907, transferred their mining rights in the district of Lingan (in which the Kochiu mines are situated) to a French company entitled Société d'Exploitation de Ling Ngan. Owing to local obstruction the company has, so far (1910), been unable to make any progress with the development of the mines. — p. 55 ([scan leaf 94](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n94/mode/1up))

**2. Ho Yuan mines** (Chinese), in Kuangsi province, near Wuchow. — p. 56 ([scan leaf 95](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n95/mode/1up))

**3. Niutsongling mines** near Nodoa (Tamchow department of Hainan). Owned by the Singapore Merchants Pioneer Company, Limited, formed in 1908. "A fairly rich stanniferous lode has been struck, the ore from which is expected to average 60 per cent per ton." — p. 56 ([scan leaf 95](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n95/mode/1up))

## Communications: Railways — Further Details

### I. State Railways

**Imperial Railways of North China (Chingfeng)** Peking to Mukden, via Tientsin and Shanhaikuan
This railway developed from the Kaiping tramway, which was constructed in 1880–1.
Capital, $49,971,571.43. Source, Anglo-Chinese.
Cost of construction: Expenditure on lines open for traffic, $47,245,485.01; expenditure on capital works from revenue, $8,903,485.29. Mileage (total), 601.

Nationality, British.
Opened to traffic, Peking to Tientsin, 1897. Main line completed 1903.
Junctions with other systems at: Loukoukiao, with Pehan Railway; Mukden and Newchwang, with South Manchuria Railway; Fengtai, with Peking-Kalgan Railway; Tientsin, with Tientsin-Pukow Railway.
Branches: Peking to Tungchow (12 miles); Fengtai to Loukoukiao (4 miles); Koupangtze to Yingkow (Newchwang) (57 miles); Tangho to Chinwangtao (6 miles), owned and controlled by C. E. and M. Co.; Lienshen to Hulutao Harbour (7 miles), now under construction.
Head office, Tientsin.
Receipts for year 1910, $10,928,242.31.
Expenditure for year 1910, $3,474,187.35.
Number of passengers carried, 3,239,158. Amount of freight, 2,752,155 tons.
Receipts from passengers, $5,141,402. From freight, $5,394,226.
Remarks: The Kaiping tramway, which was completed in 1881, from Tongshan to Hsukochuang, a distance of 7 miles, was converted into a railway and extended to Lutai, Tongku, Tientsin and eventually to Peking, in a westerly direction, and to Shanhaikuan, Kaopangtzu and Hsinmintun in a north-easterly direction. In 1907 the railway purchased the Japanese light railway between Hsinmintun and Mukden, which has since been converted to standard gauge, so that through trains now run between Peking and Mukden.
The ratio of working expenses to earnings in 1910 was 31.7 per cent. — p. 171 ([scan leaf 210](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n210/mode/1up))

## Education: Government Institutions at Peking

### Government Institutions at Peking

**The College of Languages** intended to train students for the translation of foreign works into Chinese. The course is five years, and six hours are devoted to study per day. Only students who can write good Chinese are admitted. The languages taught are English, French, Russian, German and Japanese, and every student must take at least one of these, in addition to such general subjects as morals, Chinese literature, history, geography, mathematics, chemistry, drawing and physical drill. There are three branches to enable students to specialize in diplomatic, economic and educational translations. After concluding their course, students are rewarded with the degree of Chujen (M.A.) and are eligible for employment in one of the Ministries with the seventh official rank. — p. 317 ([scan leaf 388](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n388/mode/1up))

**Nobles' Military College** established at Peking, provides a two years' course of military education for direct descendants of officials above the second rank and princes. The German language and the German system of military drill are taught. Other military schools will be found under "Army." — p. 318 ([scan leaf 389](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n389/mode/1up))

**Nobles' Law College** provides a two years' course of law for sons of princes and the nobility. — p. 318 ([scan leaf 389](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n389/mode/1up))

**The Fapu (Ministry of Laws) Law College** has for its object the teaching of law to students who have already obtained official rank and are awaiting appointment, with a view to providing the judges and law officials required under the new system. The course is three years, and besides various branches of Chinese and foreign law, it includes tuition in one foreign language. — p. 318 ([scan leaf 389](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n389/mode/1up))

**The Tuchihpu (Ministry of Finance's) College** provides courses in economics and finance for candidates for employment in the Ministry, in the Customs and in the Taching or Government Bank. Students are admitted from the junior grades of officials in the employment of the Ministry, and also from among those who have finished the High School course. The course in this college is three years. Students must be over sixteen and under twenty-five years of age at the time of admission. English is taught in this school. — p. 318 ([scan leaf 389](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n389/mode/1up))

**Police Schools** —These schools, which are under the Minchengpu (Ministry of the Interior), are of two grades, and are intended for the training of officers for the police. Lower Schools are supposed to be established in each Fu or prefecture, and a Higher School should be organized in each provincial capital. The curriculum includes general and police law, local administration, geography, English or Japanese and drill. After completing the Higher School course, the student has one year's practical instruction in the police force before being appointed as an officer. — p. 318 ([scan leaf 389](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n389/mode/1up))

**Manchu and Mongolian Languages School** —This school, established in Peking, is intended to preserve the mother tongue of the dynasty and the Mongolian language. In a subordinate department Tibetan is taught. The complete course is five years, at the expiration of which successful candidates are eligible for appointment in the Lifanpu (Ministry of Dependencies). Japanese and Russian are optional subjects. — p. 318 ([scan leaf 389](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n389/mode/1up))

**Chinghua College** (near Peking), founded to provide a preparatory course for students proceeding to America to study under the arrangement made when the American Government remitted a portion of the Boxer Indemnity. The teachers are Americans. — p. 318 ([scan leaf 389](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n389/mode/1up))

**The Yuchuanpu (Ministry of Posts and Communications') College** gives courses of instruction in railway and postal administration. — p. 318 ([scan leaf 389](https://archive.org/details/china-year-book-1912/page/n389/mode/1up))
